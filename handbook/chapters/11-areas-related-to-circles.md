## Chapter 11: Areas Related to Circles

### A. Chapter overview
Circle measures extend to sectors, arcs, segments, and composite shapes. A sector is a fraction of the full circle determined by its central angle.

**Objectives:** calculate circumference, area, arc length, sector area, segment area, and composite perimeter.

### B. Important concepts and explanations
A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc. A segment is bounded by a chord and its corresponding arc. Segment area is sector area minus the triangle formed by the radii and chord.

### C. Formula and theorem sheet
| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Circumference | $2\pi r$ |
| Circle area | $\pi r^2$ |
| Arc length | $\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}2\pi r$ |
| Sector area | $\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2$ |
| Segment area | Sector area minus triangle area |

### D. Solved board-style examples
**Example 1: Circumference and area**

**Question:** Find circumference and area for radius 7 cm, using $\pi=22/7$.

**Concept used:** Circle formulas.

**Solution:**
1. Circumference $=2(22/7)(7)=44$ cm.
2. Area $=(22/7)(49)=154$ cm$^2$.

**Final answer:** **44 cm; 154 cm$^2$.**

**Exam tip:** Circumference uses linear units; area uses square units.

**Example 2: Sector**

**Question:** Find the area and arc length of a $90^\circ$ sector of radius 14 cm, using $\pi=22/7$.

**Concept used:** Quarter-circle proportions.

**Solution:**
1. Area $=\frac14\pi(14)^2=154$ cm$^2$.
2. Arc length $=\frac14(2\pi\cdot14)=22$ cm.

**Final answer:** **Area 154 cm$^2$; arc 22 cm.**

**Exam tip:** Use the same angle fraction for arc and area.

**Example 3: Perimeter of a sector**

**Question:** Find the perimeter of a $60^\circ$ sector of radius 21 cm, using $\pi=22/7$.

**Concept used:** Two radii plus arc.

**Solution:**
1. Arc length $=(60/360)(2\pi\cdot21)=22$ cm.
2. Perimeter $=21+21+22=64$ cm.

**Final answer:** **64 cm.**

**Exam tip:** Sector perimeter includes both radii; arc length alone is not perimeter.

### E. Common examination mistakes
- Using diameter where radius is required.
- Forgetting angle fraction $\theta/360^\circ$.
- Reporting area in linear units.
- Omitting radii from sector perimeter.
- Forgetting to subtract the triangle for a segment.

### F. Exam tips and problem-solving strategy
Identify whether the boundary includes straight radii or only curved arc. Mark the central angle and radius. Keep $\pi$ exact until the end unless a value is specified.

### G. Quick revision summary
Circle area is $\pi r^2$; circumference is $2\pi r$. Sector and arc are the corresponding fraction $\theta/360^\circ$ of the whole.
